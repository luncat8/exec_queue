# 0.4.0 — orbital ports and colony growth (worklog)

## review

The 0.3.0 root demo has fixed ports, so positions are a constant table, and
ships precompute distance at departure. Two things did not exist yet: a port
whose position is a function of time (and therefore a rendezvous problem), and
any notion of the network growing. The renderer also assumed a static world:
the cached backdrop paints port glows and names once per resize, which would
smear for moving ports.

## design decisions

- **Ellipse, closed form.** `pos(t)` is evaluated directly from phase and
  angular speed instead of integrating velocity. A straight-line route is then
  solved by fixed-point iteration on the arrival time (12 passes, converges to
  machine epsilon). The cached aim point is compared with the port position at
  arrival so any future drift shows up in the HUD as `aim error`; it reads
  `0.000 px`.
- **The planet stays put, the station orbits.** Empty planets must be visible
  before they are settled, and a colony needs a destination that does not move,
  so only the station (the trading post) rides the ellipse. The page draws a
  tether from planet centre to station, which makes the motion readable.
- **Static backdrop.** The new page keeps only stars and a grid in the cached
  backdrop layer; planets, orbits, stations, ships and every moving label are
  drawn per frame. Names left the backdrop, so they can be attached to moving
  stations later without reworking the cache.
- **Nearest N at tick time, not per frame.** Partner lists are recomputed every
  3 sim minutes (and when the slider moves) into a flat array with an in-place
  insertion sort over scratch lists.
- **One ship per port, two-leg trade.** The outbound leg sells the port's own
  good to the partner with the lowest stock of it; the return leg brings the
  partner's own good home. Both deliveries grow the receiving port's ring, so
  the ring measures trade, not production.
- **Colony as a reservation.** Claiming a planet flips its state and decrements
  the empty count at launch; the ship is a second, non-trading object. This
  keeps the invariant `empty + reserved + ports = planets` checkable.
- **Expansion pace** tuned in the headless sim before touching the renderer:
  `EXPAND_COST 650`, start rings 160–420, 45–75 min cooldown after a launch,
  90–150 min grace for a fresh colony, radius 420, builder speed 44. Result:
  ~30–35 minutes per colony, full map at ~15 sim hours, queue peak 27.

## changes

- `orbit_trade.html`, `orbit_sim.js`, `orbit_gui.js` — new page, simulation and
  renderer; the page has its own palette and uses the shared `exec_queue.js`.
- `orbit_sim.js` exposes `portX/portY/planetXAt/planetYAt`, partner lists,
  builder state and a log ring, and guards `module.exports` so node can load it.
- Renderer draws: dashed orbits with a fading trail, links, empty/conquered/
  reserved planets, tethered stations, growth rings, trading triangles with
  cargo-sized hulls, colony diamonds and target pulses, hover inspector with
  the port's radius circle, links and the target it would colonise next.
- Controls: pause, speed ×0.5–12, links 1–6, reset, and toggles for orbits,
  links and routes. Hover is the only inspection interaction; no click state.
- `experiments/orbit_check.js`, `experiments/orbit_gui_smoke.js`,
  `experiments/orbit_measure.js`, `experiments/logs/orbit.log`.

## validation

- `node experiments/orbit_check.js` — planets partition into
  empty/reserved/ports, port slots stay contiguous, one ship per port, sailing
  ships always target an active port with a future arrival, cached aim equals
  `portX/Y` at arrival exactly, nearest-N equals a sorted brute-force list for
  every port at two points in the run, colony targets are inside the radius and
  reserved, jump equality for 900 minutes at 1/36/900-minute steps, and twelve
  or more colonies by minute 1500.
- `node experiments/orbit_gui_smoke.js` — stub DOM and canvas; HUD text, log
  rows, colony launch, pause/play, speed and link sliders, toggles, hover and
  pointer leave, resize with DPR, reset and space bar.
- `node experiments/orbit_measure.js` — five seeds, 15 sim hours: 18 colonies
  each, 30–35 min per colony, `aim 0`, queue peak 27. 3600 realistic frames
  (60 fps × speed 3) cost 1.5 ms in the sim (0.4 µs/frame). A 24,000-minute run
  stays at 26 ports with a queue of 27 and no backlog.
- Rendering checked with `@napi-rs/canvas` (installed outside the repo) at DPR 1
  and DPR 2, plus a hover frame: no sheared backdrop, no clipped card, trails
  and rings visible.

## notes

- The `smooth` option of the old demo was render-only easing; here the motion
  itself is smooth and exact, so no easing layer was added.
- `pos(t)` costs two `Math.sin`/`Math.cos` per port per frame; the partner
  refresh does the same per pair, once every three minutes.

## next

- Reroute a trading ship from its current position while the target keeps
  moving: this is the same fixed-point solve but starting from a moving ship,
  and it would exercise a third use of the queue (invalidate and reschedule).
- Separate colony ships from the trading fleet so a port can keep trading while
  its builder is in flight (currently the ship stays home and the ring resets).
