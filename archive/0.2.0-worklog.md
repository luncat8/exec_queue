# 0.2.0 — worklog

## steps

1. **Archived the previous root implementation.** `index.html`, `exec_queue.js`,
   `sim.js`, `gui.js`, `style.css` and its `experiments/` moved to `A2026/`
   unchanged. It stays runnable on its own (`cd A2026 && node
   experiments/gui_smoke.js`) and keeps its own benchmark logs for comparison.
2. **Compared the four builds** — `archive/0.2.0-comparison.md`. Ran each of
   `Beam1`, `GLM5.3`, `GPT6Luna` headless under a stub DOM to measure them
   instead of reading them: Beam1 keeps 12 ships all travelling with no event in
   the past, GLM5.3 settles at 26 ships and a queue of ~28, GPT6Luna's log
   reached 158k DOM rows in two minutes.
3. **Fixed the defects that were found** (list and reasoning in the comparison
   file): Beam1 retina backing store, dead fleet slider, per-frame
   `toArray().sort()`, one-minute retry polling, guard/clock desync; GLM5.3
   temp-array swaps, 220/260 stock cap mismatch, per-event DOM rows; GPT6Luna
   unbounded log, per-frame `querySelector`, guard/clock desync. During the
   GLM5.3 conversion I broke its sift-up (compared the parent instead of the
   event) — the ordering assertion in the A/B benchmark caught it immediately.
4. **Benchmarked the queues** (`experiments/queue_ab.js`, logs in
   `experiments/logs/queue-ab.log`): three workloads, identical event stream,
   each queue driven the way its own demo drives it. Typed arrays lead by
   1.4–3.4x; at the ~36 events these demos really queue, every implementation
   is in the 14–25M ops/s range, so the win is headroom and no GC, not frame
   rate.
5. **Built the best-of in root.** A2026's queue, sim and renderer as the base;
   from GLM5.3 the event log and the scarcity-aware destination choice; from
   Beam1 the fleet control, implemented live rather than dead; per-ship hold and
   speed variety. Rejected: GLM5.3's `seq` FIFO, day/night tint and route lines
   (frame cost, no information), GPT6Luna's light theme and stock chart (the
   per-port bars already show stock).
6. **Added the missing sink.** Consumption ticks are the third event kind and
   the main quality change: supply is now a flow instead of a pile. Tuning run
   (20 windows of 1,000 sim minutes, seed 11) holds 50–55% average fill for 20,000
   minutes with a stable 24–30 ship fleet and a steady 36-event queue.
7. **Clean CSS.** The old `style.css` repeated a block of rules inside its media
   query; the new one is written out, and the fleet slider and log panel are
   styled with it.
