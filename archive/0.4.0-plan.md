# 0.4.0 — orbital ports and colony growth

## goal

Two things the sea-trade demo cannot show:

1. **Moving ports, no per-frame route work.** A station rides a fixed ellipse
   around its planet, so `pos(t)` is closed-form. A voyage solves the intercept
   once at departure and then draws a straight line. Nothing is integrated per
   frame and no route is recomputed while a ship is in flight. (The contrast
   with the earlier `smooth positions` easing: that was render-only interpolation
   over a static port table.)
2. **Empty planets and organic expansion.** Most planets start without a port.
   Trade fills a growth ring; a full ring sends a colony ship to the nearest
   empty planet inside a radius, and that planet opens its own port.

The page is `orbit_trade.html` in the repo root; it shares `exec_queue.js` and
keeps its own simulation and renderer.

## port motion

Station position for planet `i` at sim time `t`:

```
a     = phase + omega * t
x(t)  = cx + rx*cos(a)*cos(tilt) - ry*sin(a)*sin(tilt)
y(t)  = cy + rx*cos(a)*sin(tilt) + ry*sin(a)*cos(tilt)
```

Per planet: centre `(cx, cy)`, semi-axes `rx, ry` (46–180 px), tilt, phase and
angular speed `omega` (period 160–400 minutes, half of them retrograde). The
ellipse is sized to fit the planet's clearance to the map edge, and the planet
itself stays static — only the station moves.

Because `pos(t)` is exact, the renderer just evaluates it for `sim.t`; there is
no velocity integration, no accumulated error and no per-frame allocation.

## rendezvous

A ship leaves `(x0, y0)` at `t0` with speed `v` and must meet a port whose
position is a function of time. Solve by fixed-point iteration:

```
arrive = t0 + |port(arrive) - start| / v      (start with port(t0))
```

Twelve iterations converge to machine epsilon for orbits this slow, so the
aim point `port(arrive)` is cached with the event (`shipAimX/Y`) and the ship
draws a straight line to it. On arrival `portX(target, arrive)` is compared to
the cached aim point: the HUD shows the residual, which stays `0.000 px`.

Rerouting or a moving ship would mean solving a new intercept from the current
position; the demo only needs the departure case.

## nearest-N trading

Each port computes its `N` nearest ports (default 4, slider 1–6) every tick.
"Nearest" is distance in the station positions at that tick; the list is
recomputed at tick time, not per frame, and trades in flight keep their target.

The partner list is a flat `Uint8Array` of `MAX_LINKS` slots per port with an
in-place insertion sort over preallocated scratch lists — no arrays allocated
per update.

Trade loop of a port's own ship:

1. **load at home** — sell the port's own good (its spec) to the partner that
   has the least of it, up to the hold and the partner's headroom. If no
   partner is worth a load, retry in 3–5 minutes.
2. **arrive** — unload into the partner (clamped to `STOCK_MAX`), growth ring
   `+= landed`.
3. **return leg** — load the partner's own good (keeping a small reserve for
   the partner's population) and fly home.
4. **arrive home** — unload, growth ring `+= landed`, schedule the next load.

So every port has exactly one ship and every leg carries a good toward a
partner that wants it. A port's ring grows from both its export sale and its
import delivery.

## economy

- Three goods: ice, ore, gas. A port produces only its spec at
  `1.35–1.65 /min` and consumes the other two at `0.66–0.74 /min`, so its
  stock of a foreign good sits at an equilibrium of roughly
  `import rate / consumption` and the "emptiest partner" choice keeps rotating.
- `STOCK_MAX 140`, hold `24`, export reserve `6`, return reserve `4`.
- Every port picks its spec as the good currently under-represented in the
  network, so a colony wave keeps the three goods balanced.

## expansion

- Planets: 26 on a generated map with a minimum spacing of 165 px. Eight are
  seeded with ports at start-up, chosen farthest-point so the initial network
  spans the map.
- Starting growth rings are 160–420 of `EXPAND_COST 650`; the rest must be
  traded for.
- A tick every 3 minutes refreshes the partner lists and, for every idle port
  with a full ring and an expired cooldown, picks the nearest empty planet
  within `EXPAND_RADIUS 420` and launches a colony ship.
- The target planet is reserved immediately (`state 2`) so two ports cannot
  claim it; the colony ship is a diamond that flies a straight line at 44 px/min
  and opens the port on arrival. The new port starts at ring 0 with a 90–150
  minute grace period; a port that just launched a colony waits 45–75 minutes,
  so no port can fire twice in a row.

Expansion pace (`experiments/logs/orbit.log`): about 30–35 minutes per colony,
1–5 colonies by hour 4, 12–15 by hour 8, all 18 by hour 15. At speed ×3 that is
a first colony within seconds and a visibly growing network in a couple of
minutes of real time.

## events

`exec_queue.js` as-is, four kinds:

| kind | ref | scheduled by | handler |
|---|---|---|---|
| `LOAD` | ship | activation, arrival home, failed load | sell to the best partner, depart |
| `ARRIVE` | ship | departure, at `t + intercept` | unload, return leg or next load |
| `TICK` | — | every 3 minutes | partner lists, colony launches |
| `BUILD` | port | colony launch | activate the reserved planet |

Production and consumption are applied by `accrue(dt)` inside the drain, so a
jump produces exactly the same world as many small steps (checked in
`experiments/orbit_check.js`).

## out of scope

- ports that wander along arbitrary random paths (the third option in the
  request); the ellipse is the predictable model, so `pos(t)` stays closed
  form
- fleet build-up, ship upgrades, combat, planetary resources
- persistence
