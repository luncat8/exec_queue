# 0.1.0 — time queue and sea-trade demo

## goal

A browser demo of a discrete-event simulation where all scheduled work lives in
a time-ordered queue: random ships carry red, green and blue goods between
ports, and the clock can jump far ahead without breaking voyage calculations.
The time slider can exceed a whole voyage, so one frame may process several
events in sequence.

## queue (`exec_queue.js`)

Binary min-heap in parallel typed arrays: `Float64Array time`, `Uint8Array kind`,
`Uint32Array ref`, integer `size`, capacity doubling.

- `push(time, kind, ref)` — sift up. O(log n), no allocation until capacity grows.
- `popRoot()` — move the last element to the root and sift down. O(log n).
- `drainUntil(target, onEvent)` — repeatedly read the root, pop it, then call
  `onEvent(time, kind, ref)`. Handlers may push follow-up events, including
  events at the same or an earlier timestamp than the target. Arrays are
  re-read through `this` each iteration because a handler can grow them.
- `peekTime()` — earliest scheduled time, `Infinity` when empty.
- `clear()`, `size`.

Equal timestamps are deliberately unordered; simultaneity has no meaning here.
A sorted array is the alternative, but insertion is O(n); see the benchmark.

## simulation (`sim.js`)

Six ports, three goods, up to 30 ships. Arrays are preallocated per ship slot;
ships retire and slot indexes are reused.

Event kinds:

- `KIND_ARRIVE` — a ship reached `shipTo[ship]`. `ref` is the ship index.
- `KIND_DISPATCH` — a new ship may be spawned. `ref` is a requested home port
  (user click) or `NO_PORT` for automatic replacement requests.

Arrival handler:

1. Unload the hold into the arrival port's stock (clamped to `STOCK_MAX`).
2. Maybe retire the ship after `RETIRE_TRIPS` trips; then schedule one dispatch
   event 5–20 minutes later.
3. Otherwise pick a good with probability proportional to its stock at the port,
   take up to `HOLD_MAX`, choose a random different destination, and schedule
   the next arrival at `eventTime + distance / SHIP_SPEED`.

The critical rule: every departure time is the **event time**, never the frame
clock. `step(minutes)` advances the clock to a target and drains all events up
to it; a handler that jumps `t` to its own timestamp lets following events run
in the same batch.

Production accrues inside `produce(time - t)` called from the event handler and
again after the drain, so supplies match the event's real time even in a jump.

Constants: `STOCK_MAX 120`, `HOLD_MAX 26`, `SHIP_SPEED 70` world px/minute,
world 1600x1000, retirement after 6 trips at 12% per arrival, initial stock
40–90, production 1.5–3.5 units/minute.

Dispatch: at most 30 ships. A request that finds the fleet full (retirement
replacement or user click) is retried 10–50 minutes later, never dropped. Ten
ships are scheduled at time 0, the rest at 3–6 minute intervals.

## demo (`index.html`, `gui.js`, `style.css`)

Full-window canvas. Static water backdrop (dots + port glows) is painted once per
resize; ships are eased toward their simulated positions so jumps read as sudden
movement instead of teleporting.

- ports: dock circle plus three supply bars (r, g, b) normalized to `STOCK_MAX`
- ships: triangle colored by cargo, size scaled by hold, heading along the leg
- HUD: clock, queue length, processed events, ships, delivered units, next event,
  and a 20-minute timeline of queued events (one tick per event, colored by
  cargo, taller for arrivals, white marker at the clock)
- controls: pause, continuous/jump mode, logarithmic speed slider (1–1000 sim
  minutes per real second), reset
- jump mode: clock advances in 30-minute steps, `30 / speed` seconds apart
- click a port to request a ship; space toggles pause; `j` jumps 30 minutes

Rendering must not allocate per frame: preallocated ship position arrays, cached
DOM elements, HUD text refreshed at 10 Hz.

## validation

- `experiments/queue_check.js` — ordering, growth, draft scenario (events at 10,
  11, 12), chained follow-ups processed by a single jump.
- `experiments/queue_bench.js` — heap vs sorted array, 50k events, checksum and
  ordering agreement.
- `experiments/sim_jump_check.js` — 1-minute steps and 37-minute steps must
  produce identical state after 6000 simulated minutes.
- `experiments/sim_events_check.js` — one queued arrival per living ship, no
  event in the past, a click against a full fleet is deferred then served.
- `experiments/sim_measure.js` — 20,000-minute run; asserts no arrival is in the
  past and samples queue length and stock fill.
- `experiments/gui_smoke.js` — stub DOM/canvas, runs frames and controls.

## out of scope

- persistence, sound, mobile layout polish
- demand/pricing, collisions, multiple event types beyond dispatch/arrival
